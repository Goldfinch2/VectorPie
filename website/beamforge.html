More speed for your vector games

BeamForge connects a Raspberry Pi running VectorPie to your cabinet’s vector monitor. With a drawing rate of approximately 20 million points per second—6.7 times the USB-DVG’s 3 million—it gives complex scenes more room to draw and supports higher frame rates where the monitor allows.

BeamForge uses the same protocol as the USB-DVG and works with the VectorPie menu, MAME games and app store games. VectorPie detects the board automatically. Configure it and update its firmware directly from the settings menu using your cabinet controls.

One board, one firmware, managed from VectorPie.

What's in the box

    Specifications

    Raspberry Pi

    Raspberry Pi 4 or Pi 5 with 1 GB memory or higher. Raspberry Pi 5 is preferred for better performance. SSD or SD card, 32 GB or higher. SSD strongly recommended. BeamForge mounts above the Pi on the GPIO header and connects to the Pi’s upper blue USB 3 port with the included data cable.

    The included 20 mm standoffs and GPIO extender are sized for a Raspberry Pi 5 with its active cooler. With a Pi 4 or a different cooler, check the clearances first.

    You also need

    • A Raspberry Pi 4 or 5, with its power supply.
    • Storage with VectorPie installed.
    • A compatible arcade vector monitor.
    • A cable from BeamForge to your monitor, not included. For a WG6100, see the DVG to Stock WG6100 cable at Vector Head Arcade. The cabinet adapters include their own vector cable.

    VectorPie

    VectorPie detects BeamForge automatically: Settings → DVG → HARDWARE shows BEAMFORGE. The menu, the MAME games and the app store games draw on it. Firmware updates install from Settings → DVG → UPDATE.... VectorPie runs on a Raspberry Pi 4 or 5. Plug the board into a USB 3.0 port (blue).

    Replacing a USB-DVG

    BeamForge uses the same protocol as the USB-DVG, so software that drives a USB-DVG drives BeamForge. Assembly, connections, monitor setup and calibration are in the BeamForge installation guide.
